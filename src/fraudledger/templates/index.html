How to read this

A score puts a provider higher or lower in the queue. It is not a probability, not a dollar amount, and not a decision that anyone did anything wrong. Config SHA-256 {{ index.config_sha }}.

Score

Each indicator has a weight. The score adds the weight once per distinct indicator, then adds the bonuses below. Peer-gap dollars are not part of the score.

score = sum of distinct indicator weights
+ {{ "%.1f"|format(index.multi_flag_bonus) }} if at least {{ index.multi_flag_min }} distinct indicators fired
+ {{ "%.1f"|format(index.r11_extra) }} if R11 fired
+ {{ "%.1f"|format(index.cluster_bonus) }} if this provider is flagged and another flagged provider is in the same cluster

TierMeaningWhen it is used
Critical Open this first. R11 fired, or the score is at least {{ "%g"|format(index.tier_critical) }}.
High Several indicators, or one heavy one, stacked up. Score is at least {{ "%g"|format(index.tier_high) }}, and it is not already Critical.
Elevated Above the noise floor. Still a lead, not a conclusion. Score is at least {{ "%g"|format(index.tier_elevated) }}.
Monitor Something fired, but not enough to sort above the rest. Score is above 0 and below Elevated.

R11 is the billing NPI on the OIG exclusion list with a payment on or after the exclusion date. Its own weight is already in the sum. The extra {{ "%.1f"|format(index.r11_extra) }} is added only for R11. With the default R11 weight of 5, that extra alone reaches the Critical cutoff. R13 is a separate owner name-and-state match. It does not get that extra, and it is not Critical by itself.

The cluster bonus is not “this provider has an owner.” It applies only when two or more flagged providers sit in the same ownership community, and that community has at most {{ index.cluster_bonus_max }} providers. A larger community does not add the bonus. The group sort key on the Groups page is a different number. A value in the hundreds there is not a provider score.

For a cost outlier, the peer-gap shown beside the score is (paid per line − peer median) × claim lines. The peer group is that provider’s state when at least {{ index.min_peer_n }} providers in the state billed the code. Otherwise it is national. It is not a loss amount and not an amount to type on an intake form.

Ownership groups

A group is providers connected by a shared owner, or by the same street address when at least three and at most {{ index.address_link_max }} providers use it. A post-office box is not used as a shared address. The group number is an id. The list is ordered so groups with more billing indicators come first.

Open a group and the page lists the shared owners and the shared street addresses by name. An owner is listed there only when that same associate is on at least two providers in the group. An address is listed when at least two providers in the group use that street, city, and state.

The group sort key is not the provider score. For the example of 167 providers with an indicator out of 687, the sort key is about 356. Almost all of that is 167 × 2. The dollar total adds its natural log, about 17 points even at $25 million. Group size adds at most 5 points. None of that means the group is “356 bad.” It means 167 members each have at least one indicator.

The peer-gap dollars on a group are those providers’ gaps added together. They are not the amount paid, and they are not a loss amount.

Indicators

Indicators are not mutually exclusive. A provider can carry several at once. R01 and R02 use a state peer group when at least {{ index.min_peer_n }} providers in that state billed the code. Otherwise they use the national group, and they skip a code when that group is also smaller than {{ index.min_peer_n }}. R16 and R17 look at the servicing NPI. R18 and R19 are Care Compare corroboration. They do not make a provider Critical by themselves. The Rules page repeats these cutoffs next to the config hash for the run.

Ownership edges

An ownership edge is one link: this provider, one PECOS associate, and one role. It is not one person, and it is not one slice of a pie. The same associate stays one owner when the name repeats.

ColumnWhat it is
PECOS associate idColumn ASSOCIATE ID – OWNER in the CMS owner file. Not an NPI. People and companies both have one. The same id is one owner even when the name is repeated, and the same id on a second provider is what links the group.
NameThe organization name, or the person’s first and last name, from that same row.
RoleThe CMS role code for this row only. The codes are in the next section.
PercentThe percent CMS stored on that role row. It is often copied onto the officer and director rows for the same stake.
Weightrole strength × percent / 100. Used only to group related providers. Not a share. Do not add the weight column.
Originpublic:home-health-owners or public:hospice-owners, the file the row came from.

Why the same name appears more than once

CMS files a separate row for every role it requires the enrollment to disclose. A person who owns 51% and is also an officer and a director is three rows, all at 51%. Those are not three stakes.

Why the percents add up past 100%

They are different layers. Add them only inside one layer, and only once per owner.

  • Direct (role 34). Equity in this provider. One owner at 100% is a complete direct total. Several direct owners should generally add to about 100%. Count each associate once. If the same associate has two role 34 rows, do not count both.
  • Indirect (role 35). Equity in a company that owns this provider. A 51% row and a 49% row can be a complete indirect layer. Do not add that 100% on top of the direct owner’s 100%.
  • Control roles (officer, director, partner, managing employee). Medicare requires these even when the person is already listed as an owner. The percent is usually the same stake again. Leave them out of the total.

What the loader changed

  • The owner file is keyed by enrollment id. Each NPI on that enrollment receives a copy of the owner row. A provider page shows the rows for that NPI only.
  • Medicaid TOTAL_PATIENTS is stored as the beneficiary count. Counts are not added across months. Rules that need a patient count use the busiest month, or the average month inside a year for the growth test.
  • Year-over-year growth is (this year paid − prior year paid) / prior year paid. If the prior year paid nothing, growth is not computed and the jump is not flagged.
  • A payment jump is dropped from R07 and R08 when both years have a patient count and the patient count grew at least {{ "%g"|format(index.census_share * 100) }}% as fast as the dollars. It is also dropped when paid per patient grew by less than {{ "%g"|format(index.rate_share * 100) }}%.
  • R05 and R06 do not flag a provider whose taxonomy is {{ index.hospice_taxonomy }} and whose billed codes are all hospice codes. They also do not flag a provider whose codes are all home-health codes, or all personal-care codes, even without that taxonomy. A home-health taxonomy billing only a hospice code is still eligible.
  • R09 requires an incorporation date or an NPPES enumeration date. A missing date is not treated as new. R09 and R10 use the incorporation date from the enrollment file when that file has one. That date is not a Medicare enrollment date. An old company with a new enrollment keeps the incorporation date, and falls back to the NPPES enumeration date only when incorporation is missing.
  • R13 matches an owner to the exclusion list on exact first name, last name, and state. When both sides have a middle name, that name must match too, including an initial against the full middle name. A person reinstated on or before the latest paid month is not flagged. The agency name is not compared. The match does not get the R11 bonus.
  • R14 and R15 use the Medicare post-acute provider file. The join is the last six digits of the enrollment CCN. When both files have a state, the state must match. The provider is compared with the median of other providers in that file, not with the Medicaid paid amount.
  • R16 flags the billing NPI when a different servicing NPI is on the exclusion list and the payment is on or after the exclusion date. R11 stays reserved for the billing NPI itself.
  • R18 and R19 use Care Compare. R18 keeps early and late live-discharge percentages. R19 keeps the home-health potentially preventable readmission rate. A score above twice the median, with at least eight peers, is corroboration. It is not a Critical tier by itself. HCRIS cost reports, SAM.gov, and state Medicaid exclusion lists are not loaded.

Federal sources

No CMS or Department of Justice form defines this score or tells you how to lay it out. Two federal sources do govern the words and the ownership arithmetic. The intake site does not publish an analytical template.
